Detailed notes:

(1) In 2018, SML Co signed a contract with a service provider outside Vietnam. The contract stipulated
that the foreign contractor is responsible for the CIT portion of FCT, amounting to VND3,000 million.
SML Co mistakenly remitted the whole contract amount without withholding the tax, and the foreign
contractor refused to return the withholding tax to SML Co. To avoid penalty, SML Co paid the full
tax amount of VND3,000 million to the tax authorities and recorded this tax as an accounting (but
non tax-deductible) expense in 2018. In a tax audit in 2019, the tax authorities accepted this
treatment.

After a long dispute in June 2020, the contractor reimbursed SML Co 60% of the tax amount. This
receipt has been recorded in SML Co's 2020 financial statements as income. The finance director
of SML Co believes that the other 40% of the tax borne on behalf of the foreign contractor is not
deductible.

(2) The company agreed to pay school fees for the children of three executives of the company: two
are foreign individuals and one is Vietnamese. According to all labour contracts, total school fees
which are to be borne by the company are capped at VND200 million per year, per employee. The
total fees paid by the company to schools during the year, supported by proper documents,
amounted to VND320 million for each executive. The total expense of VND960 million has been
recognised in the accounting profit for the year ended 30 September 2020.

The excess was received from the executives in October 2020, but SML Co’s finance director
accrued the excess income in the accounting records for the year ended 30 September 2020.
Although it is included in the accounting profit for this year, the finance director thinks the excess is
taxable in the year of receipt, so he has deducted it from taxable income this year.
