  Type 2 — Which TWO procedures best address a specific risk?
  Read the requirement carefully — it will specify the assertion or risk. Eliminate options that address a different assertion. Prioritise procedures that are direct, evidence-rich (third-party >
  internal > analytical).

2.2 Common Exam Mistakes
  Procedure too vague to score. 'Vouching to supporting documents' scores zero. Name the document. State the purpose. Use an ISA verb.
  Wrong direction of testing. Book → floor = existence. Floor → book = completeness. Mixing these is a fundamental error.
  Test of control instead of substantive procedure. Tests of control test whether a CONTROL works. Substantive procedures test whether a BALANCE is correct. Never mix them.
  Cut-off tested using the invoice, not the GDN. Revenue cut-off must be tested from the despatch date (GDN), not the invoice date. Performance obligation is satisfied on despatch.
  Analytical procedure used for fraud items. Analytical procedures are ineffective for one-off fraudulent transactions — they are already embedded in the totals. Use tests of detail for
  specific fraud risks.
  Forgetting the disclosure procedure. Exam questions frequently award one mark for reviewing disclosures against the relevant accounting standard checklist. Always include it.
  'Increase professional scepticism' without a specific procedure. This scores 0.5 marks alone. Must be followed by a specific additional test: 'focus on testing X by performing Y.'
  Management representations as primary evidence. Written representations are supplementary evidence, not a substitute for procedures the auditor could perform directly.

                 describes an outcome, not a procedure.

 3      C        Valuation = the balance is stated at the correct recoverable amount. Reviewing aged balances identifies potentially irrecoverable debts; post-year-end cash receipts confirm
                 actual recovery. Options A and B test existence; D tests accuracy/completeness of the ledger.

 4      B        Occurrence (payroll) = only real, current employees are paid for hours actually worked. Tracing from payroll to HR records and timesheets directly addresses ghost employees
                 and inflated hours. Option A tests completeness/accuracy; C is analytical; D tests accuracy of payment amounts.

Section B — Model Answer | Rigel Manufacturing Co
Part (a) — Revenue (long-term contracts)

 Substantive Procedures — Revenue [5 marks]

 1. Obtain the revenue schedule and agree the total to the trial balance and draft financial statements. Cast the schedule to confirm arithmetical accuracy.

 2. For standard contract sales, select a sample of sales invoices around the year end and agree the date of despatch per the GDN to the date of revenue recognition, to test cut-off.

 3. For each of the three long-term contracts, obtain the contract documentation and agree the contract value, start date and expected completion date to the revenue recognition schedule.

 4. Discuss with management the basis for the 65% completion rate applied to each long-term contract. Inspect supporting documentation (e.g. engineers' progress reports, project milestone
 records, costs incurred to date vs total expected costs) to assess the reasonableness of the estimate.

 5. Perform a proof-in-total for each long-term contract: independently recalculate the percentage of completion using costs incurred to date ÷ total expected costs. Compare to
 management's 65% estimate and investigate any significant differences.

 6. Apply heightened professional scepticism to the long-term contract estimates given the absence of independent review and the potential incentive to accelerate revenue recognition.

Part (b) — Inventory

 Substantive Procedures — Inventory [5 marks]

 1. Obtain the final inventory listing and cast it to confirm arithmetical accuracy; agree the total to the general ledger and financial statements.

 2. Because the count was performed two days before year end, review the continuous inventory records for movements between 28 June 20X6 and 30 June 20X6 (year end). Confirm that
 additions and despatches in this period have been correctly included in or excluded from the year-end balance.
