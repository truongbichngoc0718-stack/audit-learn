## Tests of control vs substantive procedures
<div class="b-grid">
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Tests of control</div>Designed to evaluate the <b>operating effectiveness of controls</b> in preventing, or detecting and correcting, material misstatement.</div>
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Substantive procedures</div>Designed to <b>detect material misstatement at the assertion level</b>. Two types: tests of details · substantive analytical procedures.</div>
</div>

### Generating procedures in the exam: AEIOU
| | Audit procedure | Description | Type of procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Analytical procedures | The evaluation of financial information by comparison to financial and non-financial data, and the investigation of significant differences and relationships which are inconsistent with other information | Substantive procedure |
| **E** | Enquiry (and confirmation) | Seeking information of knowledgeable persons throughout the entity or outside the entity (enquiry) and obtaining representations directly from a third party (confirmation) | Enquiry: both · Confirmation: substantive |
| **I** | Inspection | Examining records, documents and tangible assets | Both |
| **O** | Observation | Looking at a process or procedure being performed by others | Tests of controls |
| **U** | Recalculation (and re-performance) | Verifying the arithmetical accuracy of documents or records, and the auditor's independent execution of procedures or controls | Recalculation: substantive · Re-performance: tests of controls |

### Analytical procedures as a substantive procedure
- **Variance analysis:** current year information compared with the prior period or budget.
- **Ratio analysis:** calculation of ratios and investigation of significant differences.
- **Proof in total:** use of interrelationships between data (financial and non-financial) to estimate an expected value, with investigation of significant differences.

**Factors to consider:** the suitability of analytical procedures to the assertion · the reliability of the data used to develop the expectation · whether the expectation is precise enough to identify a material misstatement · the amount of difference acceptable without further investigation.

**Tests of details** gather evidence through detailed inspection of invoices, documents and assets: inspection of invoices · physical inspection of non-current assets and inventory · review of board minutes for evidence of provisions · review of after-date monies received per the cash book.

## Completeness of payables: control test or substantive test?
<div class="b-grid">
  <div class="b-card" style="border-top:4px solid var(--bisc-navy-2)"><div class="b-kicker">Tests of control · does the control operate effectively?</div>
  <ul><li>Enquire from management the process used to reconcile supplier statements.</li><li>For a sample of supplier statement reconciliations, inspect the reconciliation for evidence it has been performed and differences investigated and resolved; verify any necessary changes have been authorised and processed.</li><li>Reperform the reconciliation to ensure it has been completed accurately.</li><li>Inspect the reconciliation to verify it has been reviewed by an appropriate level of management.</li><li>Observe a supplier statement reconciliation being performed.</li></ul>
  <small>Conclusion depends on the <b>number of times</b> the control did not operate.</small></div>
  <div class="b-card" style="border-top:4px solid var(--bisc-emerald)"><div class="b-kicker">Substantive procedures · is the balance complete?</div>
  <ul><li>Perform analytical procedures on the amounts owed to major suppliers at the year end compared to the prior period; enquire about significant differences.</li><li>Circularise a sample of year-end payables to confirm the balance owed.</li><li>Inspect post year end cash book payments to suppliers to ensure the year-end liability is accurately recorded.</li><li>Calculate the payables payment ratio and compare to the prior period; discuss significant differences.</li></ul>
  <small>Conclusion depends on the <b>monetary value</b> of errors.</small></div>
</div>

<Eyebrow>Directional testing</Eyebrow>
## Errors from the records, omissions from outside
<div class="b-grid">
  <div class="b-card" style="border-top:4px solid var(--bisc-rose)"><div class="b-kicker">Tests designed to discover errors</div>Start with the <b>accounting records</b> and go to supporting documents or other evidence. Detects overstatement, and understatement through causes other than omission.<br/><small><b>Test debit items</b> (expenditure or assets) <b>for overstatement</b>: a $1,000 non-current asset entry is overstated if it should be less, the company does not own it, or it does not exist.</small></div>
  <div class="b-card" style="border-top:4px solid var(--bisc-emerald)"><div class="b-kicker">Tests designed to discover omissions</div>Start <b>outside the accounting records</b> and match back to them. Omissions are never revealed by starting with the account itself.<br/><small><b>Test credit items</b> (income or liabilities) <b>for understatement</b>: select a GDN and agree the sale is recorded in revenue, at full value ($1,000 recorded as $900 = $100 understatement).</small></div>
</div>

Directional testing derives from **double entry**: a misstated debit means a misstated credit, or an opposite misstatement of another debit. So testing one side gives evidence about the other. **Choose the direction** for each procedure below.

In ACCA wording the verb is often **trace** in both directions: "Tracing from records to physical assets (book to floor) tests EXISTENCE" (CP4 practice Q3). What scores is the **starting point**, not the verb. Branch A calls the two directions *vouching* and *tracing*.

## How this is tested
**Type 1 — Describe substantive procedures** for an account area: start with an overall/analytical procedure, then tests of detail targeting the assertions flagged in the scenario. **Type 2 — Which TWO procedures best address a specific risk?** Eliminate options that address a different assertion; prefer direct, evidence-rich procedures (third-party > internal > analytical).

| Trap | What scores |
|---|---|
| Wrong direction of testing | Book → floor = existence. Floor → book = completeness. Mixing these is a fundamental error. |
| Test of control instead of substantive procedure | Tests of control test whether a CONTROL works. Substantive procedures test whether a BALANCE is correct. |
| Analytical procedure used for fraud items | One-off fraudulent transactions are embedded in the totals. Use tests of detail for specific fraud risks. |
| Analytical procedure with a weak expectation | Most effective when data gives a precise expectation, e.g. units sold × price (CP4 mock Q8). |
| Management representations as primary evidence | Supplementary evidence only, not a substitute for procedures the auditor could perform directly. |

<IfSkipped lang="en">the direction of testing, you can test every recorded liability perfectly and still miss the unrecorded ones that make payables materially understated.</IfSkipped>

<KeyPrinciple>**Pick the assertion first**, then the source and direction of evidence: errors from the records, omissions from outside them.</KeyPrinciple>

<QuickCheck lang="en" q="An assistant reviews the payables ledger, selects 25 recorded balances and agrees each to supplier invoices. Will this find unrecorded liabilities?" short="No">
Starting from the ledger can only test what is recorded (existence, accuracy). Omissions are found by starting outside the records: post-year-end payments matched back to the year-end payables listing (completeness).
</QuickCheck>
