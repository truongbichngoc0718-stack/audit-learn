<Eyebrow>Definition · ISA 500</Eyebrow>
## Evidence is everything behind the opinion
> **Audit evidence** is all the information used by the auditor in arriving at the conclusions on which the auditor's opinion is based (ISA 500: para. 5(c)).

The auditor should obtain **sufficient appropriate audit evidence** to be able to draw reasonable conclusions on which to base the audit opinion (ISA 500: para. 14).

<Eyebrow>Financial statement assertions · ISA 315</Eyebrow>
## Two families of assertions
Assertions are representations by management, explicit or otherwise, that the auditor uses to consider the different types of potential misstatement. Evidence must be relevant to the particular assertion being tested. Click each family to open it.

**Management's expert.** An individual or organisation possessing expertise in a field other than auditing or accounting, whose work is used by the entity to assist in the preparation of the financial statements (for example a chartered surveyor). To rely on that work, ISA 500 requires the auditor to evaluate the **competence, capabilities and objectivity** of the expert, obtain an understanding of the work done, and evaluate its appropriateness as audit evidence.

<IfSkipped lang="en">the assertion, you can gather plenty of reliable evidence that answers the wrong question: a bank letter proves the bank's balance, not the cash book's.</IfSkipped>

<KeyPrinciple>Evidence counts only if it is **relevant to the assertion** and **reliable for its source**; the quantity needed depends on risk and materiality.</KeyPrinciple>

<QuickCheck lang="en" q="An auditor obtains a bank confirmation letter for the client's only bank account. Is that sufficient evidence for the year-end bank balance?" short="Not on its own">
The bank letter will not give the auditor all the information required. There will need to be consideration of the bank reconciliation as well. Two sources of evidence combine to be sufficient.
</QuickCheck>
