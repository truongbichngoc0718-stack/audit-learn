**Account schedule (Ch 12):** OB + additions (purchase, self-construction completed, revaluation, reclassification) − reductions (depreciation, impairment, disposal, reclassification) = balance, supported by the FA register and a physical count. Key assertions: existence (additions acquired; recorded assets in use) · completeness (all additions and disposals recorded) · rights and obligations · accuracy, classification and valuation (cost less accumulated depreciation).

## Audit plan by assertion (Ch 12 §2)

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Existence · completeness">

**Existence:** confirm the company inspects all register items each year · inspect assets, concentrating on high-value items and additions: exist, in use, in good condition, correct serial numbers · review records of income-yielding assets · reconcile vehicles by number as well as amount.

**Completeness:** summary reconciling gross book value, accumulated depreciation and net book value with the opening position · compare the GL with the register · **for a sample of assets that physically exist, agree they are in the register** · if no register, schedule of original cost and depreciated value · reconcile the schedule with the GL.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Rights · additions · disposals">

**Rights and obligations:** title deeds, land registry certificates, leases · certificate from solicitors/bankers (custody only; free from mortgage or lien) · vehicle registration documents in the client's name · documents of title for other assets · charges in statutory books / company search · lease covenants · invoices after the year end, orders and minutes for **capital commitments**.

**Additions** (rights, valuation, completeness): architects' certificates, completion statements, invoices · **review repairs, motor and sundry expenses for capital items** (capital/revenue distinction, consistent policy) · grants claimed and received · additions recorded in the register and GL. **Self-constructed:** materials, labour and overheads to invoices and wage records; capitalise only enhancements, replaced components or major overhauls; no profit element; finance costs capitalised consistently and not exceeding total finance costs.

**Disposals** (rights, completeness, accuracy): supporting documents for title transfer, price and dates · recalculate profit or loss · proceeds reasonable · release from security.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Valuation · depreciation">

**Revaluation:** agree to the valuation certificate · assess the valuer (experience, scope, methods, assumptions, basis in line with standards) · reperform the surplus · full valuation every five years, interim in year three · losses to SPL unless a credit in equity for that asset; gains to equity · insurance values and expiry dates.

**Depreciation:** review rates against asset lives, residual values, replacement policy, past gains/losses on disposal, consistency, obsolescence · all limited-life assets depreciated · revalued assets on the revalued amount · reperform · ratio of depreciation to cost by category vs prior years and policy · policy disclosed. **Classification:** IAS 16 disclosures; no further charge on fully depreciated assets.

**Accounting estimates (ISA 540):** useful life is an estimate, like the allowance for receivables, litigation provisions, inventory obsolescence, accrued revenue and warranties. Obtain evidence that estimates and disclosures are reasonable.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Intangibles · R&D">

**Research** cannot be capitalised. **Development** must be capitalised if ALL the IAS 38 criteria are met: **P**robable future economic benefits · **I**ntention to complete and use/sell · **R**esources adequate and available · **A**bility to use/sell · **T**echnical feasibility · **E**xpenditure measured reliably.

Procedures: confirm capitalised costs meet IAS 38 by inspecting project details and discussing with technical managers · feasibility and viability from budgets · recalculate amortisation, starting with production · inspect invoices for R&D spend. **Goodwill:** agree consideration to the agreement; review impairment with management. **Other intangibles:** agree to purchase documents; inspect specialist valuations; recalculate amortisation.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

<Eyebrow>Activities · Dress You Like Co</Eyebrow>
**Activity 3 · Additions ($176,000).** Includes cutting machinery $15,000 + set-up $2,500 + **training $2,500**; sewing equipment **$45,000** (deposit $20,000 paid 1/09/20X6, balance on delivery 10/10/20X6); 4 delivery vehicles $80,000.
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
**Training costs** ($2,500) are not allowed in the cost of an asset under IAS 16: otherwise NCA overstated and expenses understated. The **sewing equipment** was not in the company's possession and use at the year end: the $20,000 deposit is a payment on account (disclose if material), and the $45,000 must be removed from additions.

**Procedures:** recalculate the listing and agree to the NCA note · trace a sample to invoices/contracts (accurate, excluding recoverable sales tax) · inspect that invoices are addressed to the company and vehicle registrations are in its name (rights) · physically verify a sample (existence, condition) · confirm inclusion in the register · review additions for items that should be expensed · review repairs, motor and sundry expenses for items that should be capitalised · examine post year-end invoices, orders and minutes for capital commitments.
</Solution>

**Activity 4 · Disposals.** Old delivery vehicles: cost $41,000 (March 20X0), accumulated depreciation $24,000 at disposal in March 20X6, sold for $10,000.
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
Obtain and cast the list of disposals; agree to the FS · confirm they are removed from the register · verify to the buyer's invoice (title, price, dates) · **recalculate the loss on disposal**: carrying amount $17,000 ($41,000 − $24,000) less proceeds $10,000 · consider whether proceeds are reasonable · recalculate depreciation to the disposal date · trace proceeds to the cash book and bank statement.

*Note: the slide prints the loss as $17,000; with a carrying amount of $17,000 and proceeds of $10,000 the arithmetic gives a loss of **$7,000**.*
</Solution>

**Activity 5–6 · Revaluation (+$1.2m) and depreciation.**
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
**Revaluation:** trace to the valuer's report ($1,200,000) · agree the transfer to the revaluation reserve · agree the basis of valuation and its disclosure · assess the specialist's competence, capability and objectivity (experience, qualifications, reputation, scope, methods, assumptions, basis per standards) · compare with similar properties recently sold · recalculate depreciation on the revalued amount · review the IAS 16 disclosure.

**Depreciation:** confirm the policy is unchanged and disclosed per IAS 16 · assess rates for reasonableness · compare depreciation/cost ratios · all limited-life assets depreciated · recalculate a sample · revalued sites on the revalued amount · or **proof in total** per category: ((opening cost + closing cost)/2) × rate, investigating differences.
</Solution>

<KeyPrinciple>**Roll it forward and look both ways across the capital/revenue line:** additions from the register to evidence, and repairs from the expense accounts back to the register.</KeyPrinciple>

<QuickCheck lang="en" q="Which procedure tests completeness of PPE: (a) inspect assets selected from the register, or (b) agree assets seen on the premises to the register?" short="(b)">
For a sample of assets which physically exist, agree that they are recorded in the register (floor → book). Starting from the register (a) tests existence (Ch 12 §2).
</QuickCheck>
