<Eyebrow>The cycle on one screen</Eyebrow>
## Two functions, three stages
**HR** appoints, removes and appraises staff and notifies salary changes; **payroll processing** runs the monthly payroll. They should be different people; small businesses may lack the segregation. Inherent risks: **fraud** (fake payroll records, unauthorised pay-rate changes, claims for hours not worked, theft of cash wages) and **errors** (tax complexity, other deductions).

<Eyebrow>Substantive playbook</Eyebrow>
## Expectation first, then the people
| Procedure (Ch 16 §2.1) | Why |
|---|---|
| Reconcile gross payroll costs to the wages cost in the FS · recast payroll records | Completeness and accuracy of the journals |
| Confirm net pay per payroll to the cheque/bank transfer summary; agree net pay per cash book to payroll | Occurrence: money left the bank for these people |
| Inspect payroll for unusual items; discuss with management | Ghosts, one-off payments |
| **Proof in total:** estimated average wage (last year + expected increases) × average number of employees (incorporating starters and leavers); investigate differences | Overall reasonableness |
| Reperform statutory deductions; recalculate gross and net pay for a sample | Accuracy |
| Agree pay for a sample to contracts | Occurrence, accuracy |
| Joiners and leavers: agree dates to contracts/resignation letters; check added/removed in the correct month; recalculate that month | Occurrence, cut-off |

**Outsourced payroll (ISA 402).** The client is a *user entity*, the payroll bureau a *service organisation*; a *service auditor* may report on its controls. The user auditor must not refer to the service auditor's work in an unmodified report.

<Eyebrow>Activity · Dress You Like Co payroll expense</Eyebrow>
Monthly payroll totals **$12,677k** in 20X5-20X6 against **$11,721k** in 20X4-20X5; November and December are much higher in both years, December 20X5 was **$1,508k**.
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
November and December are significantly higher in both periods and need investigation. The increase could be legitimate (bonuses, overtime, temporary staff for a busy period). December 20X5 at $1,508,000 is **28% higher** than the prior December and must be investigated. Excluding November and December, payroll increased by **6%**: find the reason (annual increases, more staff) rather than a misstatement.

**Procedure:** analytical procedures on the monthly payroll expense compared with the prior year; investigate significant differences. **Also:** cast the schedule · agree the total to the TB · agree prior year to prior FS and audit file · agree monthly totals to the wages system and to the bank statements and cash book · compare with budget.
</Solution>

<Eyebrow>Directors' emoluments · Ch 17 §4</Eyebrow>
## Material by nature
| Assertion | Procedures |
|---|---|
| **Existence** | Agree the disclosure to a schedule per director split into salary, bonus, benefits, pension and other amounts · vouch salary and pension to monthly payroll and bank · vouch bonuses and one-off payments to board minutes, payroll and bank |
| **Rights & obligations** | Verify to contracts of employment · board / remuneration committee minutes for bonuses and authorisation · review cash book during and after the year for significant sums |
| **Completeness** | Written confirmation from directors that disclosed emoluments are complete · analytical review per director vs prior year and expectations |
| **Accuracy** | Recast the schedule; agree to the FS |
| **Presentation** | Agree to returns to tax authorities for benefits · review the note against standards and local law |

**Activity 4:** G Lewis (Chairman) $250,000 · K Escombe (CFO) $162,000 · M Batter (CEO, joined in the year) $41,500 including a $10,000 incentive payment; total $453,500.
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
**Salary:** vouch to monthly payroll and bank · for M Batter, obtain her start date from HR, vouch to board minutes and recalculate her salary pro rata. **Pension:** vouch to payroll and bank. **Bonuses:** vouch to board minutes, payroll and bank (authorised and accurate). **Incentive payment:** review M Batter's contract for the clause; vouch to board minutes, payroll and bank. **General:** recast the schedule; review the disclosure against law and standards.
</Solution>

<Eyebrow>Exam angle</Eyebrow>
| Requirement | What scores |
|---|---|
| Payroll deficiencies (CP3 Solaris) | No formal leaver process → ex-employees paid, payroll overstated, ghost employees → formal leaver form on the last day + monthly independent review of the payroll listing |
| Substantive procedures on wages | Proof in total first, then net pay to bank, joiners/leavers, recalculations |
| Directors' emoluments | Schedule per director, board minutes, contracts, written representation, disclosure |

<IfSkipped lang="en">the proof in total, a ghost employee paid every month blends into a payroll that "looks normal" line by line; only the expectation (headcount × average pay) shows the excess.</IfSkipped>

<KeyPrinciple>**Payroll = people × rate × time:** build that expectation, then prove the people exist (HR files, joiners/leavers) and the money reached them (bank).</KeyPrinciple>

<QuickCheck lang="en" q="Why are directors' bonuses tested even when the amount is below performance materiality?" short="They are material by nature">
Directors' emoluments are a sensitive area and deemed material by their nature, so the disclosure must be accurate (Ch 17 §4; CP2 mock M6).
</QuickCheck>
