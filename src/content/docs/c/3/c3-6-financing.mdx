| Objective | Example tests (Ch 17 §1) |
|---|---|
| **Existence · rights** | Direct confirmation from banks and lenders of amounts outstanding, accrued interest and security held |
| **Completeness** | Breakdown vs prior year working papers; items no longer included agreed to repayment in the cash book or reclassification to current · board minutes for new borrowings · cash book for new loans |
| **Accuracy, valuation** | Proof in total of finance charges · agree capital and interest to confirmations · recalculate finance charges using rates in the loan agreements |
| **Classification · presentation** | Current/non-current split agreed to repayment dates in the agreements · disclosure checklist |

**Risk → response (Ch 5):** significant borrowing and cash-flow problems may indicate going concern problems → review correspondence with the lender for withdrawal or extension of facilities · review **covenant** compliance and increase testing where management could manipulate the measures (e.g. provisions) · review post year-end results and cash flow forecasts. CP2 Blackberry: net assets $8.5m vs an $8m covenant → flag **both** manipulation risk (assets overstated, liabilities understated) **and** going concern.

**Ch 17 Activity 1.** Schedule: loan with CR Bank $2,597k (20X5: $2,896k). *Which TWO procedures give sufficient appropriate evidence over the COMPLETENESS of this schedule?* A. Review board minutes for discussions regarding long term borrowings · B. Agree the loan interest rate to the loan agreement with CR Bank · C. Review prior year audit working papers for any non-current liabilities no longer included · D. Trace loan repayments to entries in the cash book.
<Solution label="Show the answer">**A and C.** Board minutes reveal new borrowings that may be unrecorded; prior year papers reveal liabilities that have dropped out of the schedule.</Solution>

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Provisions · contingencies">

**Definitions (IAS 37):** provision = liability of uncertain timing or amount · liability = present obligation from past events, settlement expected to cause an outflow · obligating event creates a **legal** (contract, legislation, operation of law) or **constructive** obligation (established practice, published policy or a specific current statement creating a valid expectation) · contingent liability = possible obligation, or present obligation not recognised because the outflow is not probable or cannot be measured reliably · contingent asset = possible asset dependent on uncertain future events.

**Audit plan:** obtain details of all provisions and contingencies · analysis of opening, movements, closing · recalculate · compare with post year-end payments and past settlements · if no reliable estimate, check a contingent liability is disclosed · would you expect other provisions (e.g. warranties)? · IAS 37 disclosures. For each material provision: **present obligation?** (correspondence; discussion with directors on valid expectations) · **probable outflow?** (after-date cash; correspondence with solicitors, banks, customers, insurers, suppliers; letter to the solicitor; history of similar provisions; reimbursement).

**Litigation (ISA 501):** enquiries of management and in-house lawyers · minutes and correspondence with external legal advisers · legal expense accounts · with management's permission, a **letter of enquiry to external legal advisers**.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Capital · dividends">

- **Share capital:** authorised capital agreed to statutory documents; changes to resolutions.
- **Issues:** to general and board minutes; within the constitution and directors' authority; consideration received, or called-up capital not paid.
- **Transfers:** correspondence, stamped transfer forms, cancelled certificates, minutes; register of members agreed to issued capital in the GL.
- **Dividends:** to minute book authority; reperform against shares issued; agree payments to returned dividend warrants; not in breach of distribution rules.
- **Reserves:** movements authorised and legal; distributable vs non-distributable identified; disclosures.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

**Activity 3 · Litigation.** The finance director, whose salary was frozen while other directors received 5%, left in March 20X6 and is suing for constructive dismissal. No provision or disclosure: the company believes the claim has no merit.
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
With management's permission, send a **letter of enquiry to the external legal advisers** for their opinion of the likely outcome and financial implications (most will not meet the auditor) · review post year-end correspondence with the legal advisers · read post year-end board minutes · review correspondence with the former director · discuss the accounting treatment with the current directors. *Follow-up (Ch 19 Activity 1): the court awards $500,000 against a $100,000 provision before the report is signed → adjusting event; provision understated by $400,000 (39% of PBT).*
</Solution>

<Eyebrow>Exam angle · CP5 Cookit Co (Mar/Jun 2024 Q3)</Eyebrow>
Closure of three stores announced publicly and to staff on **28 May** (YE 31 May); redundancy cost **$1.8m** not provided. PBT $22.7m, total assets $85m.

| Step | Model answer (CP5) |
|---|---|
| Procedures | Cast the schedule and agree to TB/FS · board minutes for the decision date · press release and staff communication (constructive obligation before YE) · recalculate a sample of entitlements from policy, service and salary; agree to HR · written representation · post-YE payments |
| Effect on the report | Constructive obligation crystallised → liabilities understated, profit overstated by $1.8m · 7.9% of PBT, 2.1% of total assets: material · confined to one area: not pervasive → **qualified "except for"**, Basis for Qualified Opinion immediately after the Opinion |

<IfSkipped lang="en">the minutes and the legal letter, the liabilities that sink companies (new loans, lawsuits, restructuring promises) never reach your schedule, because they were never posted.</IfSkipped>

<KeyPrinciple>**Financing liabilities are found in words before numbers:** minutes, agreements, announcements and lawyers' letters tell you what should be on the balance sheet.</KeyPrinciple>
