## Hunting for what is not there
Auditors should be particularly aware of the possibility of **understatement of liabilities** to improve liquidity and profits (by understating the corresponding purchases). Two detailed objectives: is there a satisfactory **cut-off** between goods received and invoices received? Do payables represent the **bona fide amounts due**? Testing for completeness is harder because you are looking for something that has not been recorded (Ch 16 §1.2).

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Payables & accruals">

| Assertion | Procedures (Ch 16 §1.6) |
|---|---|
| **Completeness** | Cast the payables listing and agree to the GL · enquire how unrecorded liabilities and accruals are identified; examine post year-end transactions · reconcile selected supplier statements · examine unmatched purchase orders and invoices · confirm a sample of payables · disclosure checklist · compare balances and amounts per supplier with the prior year · payables payment period vs prior year and industry |
| **Existence** | Vouch selected balances and accruals to purchase orders and invoices · supplier statements · confirmation · analytical comparison with the prior year |
| **Rights & obligations** | Vouch a sample of balances to purchase orders and invoices |
| **Valuation** | Trace to supporting documents (orders, minutes, invoices) · supplier statements · **recalculate a sample of accruals** · compare with the prior year · recalculate invoices |
| **Cut-off** | Compare voucher dates with recording dates · test transactions around the year end · purchase returns as % of sales or cost of sales vs prior year |

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Supplier statements">

For selected suppliers: obtain statements at the year end → compare with the client's records → seek explanations for differences.

**Reasons for differences:** timing (invoices not yet received by the client; payments not yet received by the supplier; returns and credit notes not yet on the statement) · errors (supplier errors that stay until corrected; client errors to adjust) · administrative (not yet processed).

| Balance per purchase ledger | xxx |
| Add: invoices not yet posted · goods received not invoiced | xxx |
| **Reconciled balance** (both routes should be the same) | xxx |

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Confirmation of payables">

Strong controls → confirm large balances. Weak controls → confirm large balances, suppliers with a **small or zero balance**, and a sample of other accounts. Use a positive **blank (zero-balance) confirmation**: it does not state the balance; the supplier declares the amount owed and sends a detailed statement. Reconcile differences (goods in transit, cash in transit, disputed invoices). Rarely carried out in practice (Ch 16 round up).

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Purchases & expenses">

- Inspect a sample of purchase invoices to the amount posted in the GL.
- Compare administrative expenses with the prior year and expectations line by line; investigate differences.
- Enquire about unsettled claims or obligations before the year end and ensure they are provided for.
- Recalculate accruals and prepayments.
- Gross profit margin vs prior year, budget and expectations.
- **Risk → response (Ch 5):** invoices received or payments made in advance/arrears → review post year-end bank statements and cash book payments for amounts relating to the year; for documents either side of the year end (e.g. GRNs) verify cost and liability are in the right period.

</TabItem>

## Supplier statements and payments after the year end
**Activity 1.** Only the main supplier (LHT Ltd, $1,300k, agrees) is reconciled each month. For the sample, total supplier statement balances are **$2,308k** against purchase ledger balances of **$2,060k**; Freda's Fabrics, Fabricated, Three Linens Ltd and Penelope Prints Shop differ.
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
The purchase ledger balances for Freda's Fabrics, Fabricated, Three Linens Ltd and Penelope Prints Shop are higher than the balances in the supplier statements and should all be investigated. *(Note: the totals show the supplier statements are higher, $2,308k vs $2,060k, which fits the understatement conclusion.)* The difference totals **$248k, which is 12% of the total purchase ledger balance**, and could be indicative of an **understatement of trade payables**.

**Procedure:** for a sample of suppliers, reconcile the year-end balance on the purchase ledger to the supplier statement and investigate any differences, to ensure completeness, existence and valuation of trade payables.
</Solution>

**Activity 2.** Payments on 1–7 October 20X6 include: Freda's Fabrics for an invoice dated 12/09/20X6 (not in the ledger) · Three Linens Ltd for an invoice dated 29/09/20X6 (not in the ledger) · Fabricated for an invoice dated 1/10/20X6 (not in the ledger) · $8,000 internet for July–September (not in accruals).
<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
The payments to Freda's Fabrics and Three Linens Ltd relate to invoices dated before the year end but not in the year-end purchase ledger: **payables and purchases are understated by $71,000**; propose an adjustment. Fabricated is correctly excluded (invoice after the year end). The **$8,000 internet** relates to the year: **accruals and expenses understated**; propose an adjustment.

**Procedure:** for a sample of post year-end payments in the cash book, determine whether they relate to the year; if so, vouch that they are included in the purchase ledger or accruals listing. This test looks for **unrecorded liabilities**.
</Solution>

## Deficiency → recommendation → test of control
| Deficiency in the scenario | Why it matters | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Exception report of supplier master file changes printed and filed, **not reviewed** | Fictitious suppliers or amended bank details go undetected → payments to the wrong party | Reviewed promptly by an official independent of those who can amend the file; investigate; sign and date |
| GRN checks **quantity only**, not quality | Defective goods accepted → hard to dispute the invoice; inventory overstated | Inspect quality and condition too; initial the GRN as evidence |
| Supplier statement reconciliations **stopped** | Duplicate payments, unposted invoices, credits not applied → payables misstated | Reinstate monthly for material suppliers; investigate reconciling items; reviewed and signed off |

**Examiner:** the deficiency is often what is NOT described ("generated and filed" = nobody reviews it) · recommendations phrased "ensure that…" score zero · tests of control test the control, substantive procedures test the balance.

<IfSkipped lang="en">the direction, you vouch every recorded payable to an invoice and sign off a balance that is $71,000 short, because the missing invoices were never in the ledger you started from.</IfSkipped>

<KeyPrinciple>**Liabilities hide by omission:** start from supplier statements, payments after the year end and unmatched GRNs, then match back to the ledger.</KeyPrinciple>

<QuickCheck lang="en" q="The same employee adds new suppliers to the master file and authorises payments to them. Which control principle has failed, and why does it matter?" short="Segregation of duties">
Creating suppliers and approving payments are incompatible functions. One person controlling both can create fictitious suppliers and pay them: one of the most commonly tested weaknesses (CP3 practice Q7).
</QuickCheck>
