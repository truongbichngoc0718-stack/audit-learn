<Eyebrow>Substantive playbook</Eyebrow>
## Bank letter + reconciliation, never one without the other
**Bank confirmation letter (ISA 505):** the auditor chooses which banks (size of balance, volume of activity, reliance on controls, materiality) · the client gives the bank **written authority** (often a standing authority; all parties for joint accounts) · letter on the auditor's paper, sent at least a month before the year end · replies **direct to the auditor**; check every item requested is answered; no negative confirmation · agree the amounts to the client's records and investigate differences.

| Assertion | Procedures (Ch 15 §2.3) |
|---|---|
| **Completeness & existence** | Standard confirmations from every bank used in the period · reperform the reconciliation · trace outstanding cheques to the pre-YE cash book and after-date statements; explain items not cleared · compare cash book and bank statements for the last month in detail; explain unmatched items |
| **Valuation** | Prior month's reconciliation items cleared or carried forward · contra items verified to original entry · **paying-in slips prove uncleared lodgements were banked before the year end** · dishonoured lodgements followed up · balances agreed to the bank letter and statements |
| **Rights & obligations** | Enquire about restrictions on accounts |
| **Classification** | Accounts secured on company assets · legal right of set-off of overdrafts against positive balances · disclosures |

**Window dressing (cut-off):** (a) keeping the cash book open to take credit for remittances received after the year end (bank up, receivables down) · (b) recording cheques in the period that are not despatched until after the year end (bank down, payables down). Together they make the current ratio look healthier. Hence: paying-in slips for uncleared lodgements; check that large numbers of outstanding cheques clear within a reasonable time.

**Cash balances:** often individually immaterial, but fraud opportunities where controls are weak, and possibly material in total → cash count at the period end: planning, count, follow-up.

<Solution label="Show the slide answer">
**Cheque 2411 ($10,250)** has a reference suggesting it was raised much earlier in the year; it is unusual that it has not cleared → enquire. Trace outstanding cheques to the pre-YE cash book and confirm they cleared on after-date statements, so the bank balance is not understated; explain any not cleared.

**Deposits 1122 and 1123:** verify on paying-in slips that they were paid in before the year end; check they cleared quickly after the year end. Any that did not could indicate **window dressing** and overstated cash.

**Other:** agree $758,389 to the statement and the bank letter · recast the reconciliation · review the August reconciliation for items cleared or carried forward · agree $750,000 to the GL and FS.
</Solution>

**Exam focus (Ch 15):** the bank letter shows the balance per the bank's records, which must be reconciled to the client's records. Candidates lose marks for not suggesting procedures on the **year-end reconciliation**.

<Eyebrow>Variant · not-for-profit organisations (Ch 18)</Eyebrow>
## Same hub, different risks
NFP entities (e.g. a hospital, a charity) aim to deliver services, not profit; many use **value for money** indicators and must show funds were used cost-effectively. Small NFPs share small-entity weaknesses: little segregation, unqualified staff, volunteers who resent formal procedures. The auditor must consider whether records reflect **all** transactions.

| | Key factors (Ch 18) |
|---|---|
| **Inherent risk** | Complex regulation · significance of donations and cash receipts · ownership and timing of voluntary income raised by non-controlled bodies · unpredictable income · restricted funds and governing documents (charities) · trading compatible with charitable status · tax complexity · sensitive statistics (e.g. share of admin costs) |
| **Control risk** | Time and skills trustees commit · frequency and content of meetings · independence of trustees · division of duties between management and trustees |
| **Controls over cash donations** | Collecting boxes: numbered, sealed, regular collection, dual control over counting · post: unopened mail secure, dual control over opening, immediate recording, paying-in slips agreed by an independent person · restricted funds: separate records, terms, independent oversight |

<QuickCheck lang="en" q="Why should the bank confirmation never be a negative confirmation?" short="A response is needed for every request">
It is important that a response is sought for all confirmation requests; auditors should not request a response only if the information is incorrect or incomplete (Ch 15 §2.1.4).
