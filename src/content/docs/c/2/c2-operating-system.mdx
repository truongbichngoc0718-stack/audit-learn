## One loop, six models
Every exam requirement in Parts B–D runs the same loop: **risk → assertion → evidence → procedure → conclusion** (CP5 calls it the Checkpoint 4 risk-driven workflow). The six tabs below are the parts of that loop. Learn them here; the [cycle playbooks](/c/1/c1-master-map/) only change the documents.

| # | Model | Question it answers | Exam use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Risk chain | What could go wrong, and where? | "Describe audit risks and the auditor's response" (2 marks each) |
| 2 | Assertions | Which claim by management is at risk? | Every procedure's WHY |
| 3 | Evidence & AEIOU | What evidence, from which source, how reliable? | Generating procedures; MCQ on reliability |
| 4 | Direction | Start from the records or from outside? | Existence vs completeness |
| 5 | Roll-forward | Does opening + movements = closing? | NCA, inventory, payables, provisions, loans |
| 6 | Proof in total | What should the number be? | Depreciation, interest, payroll, warranty |

<TabItem label="1 · Risk chain">

### Audit risk = inherent risk × control risk × detection risk
- **Inherent risk:** the susceptibility of an assertion to a misstatement that could be material, before consideration of any related internal controls. Affected by the industry, regulation, complex calculations and standards, and estimates (ISA 200).
- **Control risk:** the risk that a material misstatement will not be prevented or detected and corrected on a timely basis by the entity's internal control.
- **Detection risk:** the risk that the auditor's procedures will not detect a material misstatement. Split into **sampling risk** and **non-sampling risk** (lack of experience, time pressure, financial constraints, poor planning, new client, lack of industry knowledge).

Detection risk is the only component the auditor controls: if IR and CR are both high, DR must be set very low → extensive substantive testing (CP2). Worked example (CP2 practice Q3): DR = AR / (IR × CR) = 4% / (80% × 50%) = **10%**.

### How to write one risk for 2 marks (CP2)
| Step | What to write | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Identify | The scenario factor that gives rise to the risk (FS level, or assertion level: completeness, existence, occurrence…) | ½ |
| 2 · Explain | The implication: which FS area, which assertion, over or understated. *E.g. Overstate inventory · Valuation* | ½ |
| 3 · Respond | The auditor's approach as a procedure: verb + noun + reason. Use specific methods, general procedures (analytical review, casting, reconcile GL to sub-ledger) or more experienced staff / larger samples | 1 |

**Notes from CP2:** answer from the **audit** perspective, not the business perspective · two facts with the same risk score once · use "misstate" only when the balance could go either way, otherwise over/understate · the response is written like a procedure.

| Scenario factor (CP2) | Audit risk identified |
|---|---|
| Company is loss-making / directors have bonus scheme based on profit | Management may overstate revenue or understate expenses to hit targets |
| New accounting system implemented during year | Risk of data migration errors; transactions may be processed incorrectly or lost |
| Significant related party transactions | May not be at arm's length; may not be properly disclosed per IAS 24 |
| Going concern doubt / bank loan covenant close to breach | Inappropriate going concern basis used; disclosures may be inadequate (ISA 570) |
| Inventory held at multiple locations / third parties | Existence and completeness of inventory unverifiable; overstatement risk |
| Director estimates involved (e.g. warranty provision) | Subjectivity in management estimates may lead to material misstatement of provisions |

Materiality sets how far down the chain you go: 0.5–1% of revenue, 5–10% of profit, 1–2% of total assets (Ch 5 §3.2), with **performance materiality** set lower to keep the aggregate of uncorrected and undetected misstatements below materiality. Branch A works the same thresholds on Biscore: [Bài 2.1 OM · PM · CTT](/a/2/a2-1-om-pm-ctt/).

<TabItem label="2 · Assertions">

### Evidence must answer the right claim
> **Audit evidence** is all the information used by the auditor in arriving at the conclusions on which the auditor's opinion is based (ISA 500: para. 5(c)).

Evidence must be **sufficient** (quantity: risk assessment, controls, materiality, previous experience, results of procedures, source and reliability) and **appropriate** (relevant to the assertion, reliable for its source). Assertions are representations by management, explicit or otherwise, that the auditor uses to consider the different types of potential misstatement. Click each family.

**Management's expert** (e.g. a chartered surveyor): to rely on the work, evaluate the expert's **competence, capabilities and objectivity**, understand the work done, and evaluate its appropriateness as audit evidence (ISA 500).

| Assertion | Key procedures (CP4) | Common trap (CP4) |
|---|---|---|
| Existence / Occurrence | Physical inspection (book → floor); third-party confirmation; post-year-end cash receipts | Book→floor = existence. Floor→book = completeness. Never mix them. |
| Completeness | Trace floor→book; sequence checks; post-year-end payment testing for liabilities | Completeness of liabilities is often MORE at risk than existence. |
| Valuation / Accuracy | Recalculate; agree to invoices/contracts; aged listings; NRV; allowance review | Recalculate = auditor does the maths. Agree = compare to source. Both needed. |
| Cut-off | GDNs around year end to invoice dates; GRNs to accrual dates; pre/post year-end journals | Revenue cut-off: test from GDN date, NOT invoice date. |
| Rights & obligations | Title documents; legal confirmations; loan agreements | Often combined with existence, but you can exist without having the right. |
| Presentation & disclosure | Disclosure checklist; classification in FS | Always include a disclosure procedure. |

<TabItem label="3 · Evidence & AEIOU">

### Two kinds of evidence
<div class="b-grid">
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Tests of control</div>Evaluate the <b>operating effectiveness of controls</b>. The control must be properly designed, exist, and have operated throughout the period (Ch 9). Conclusion depends on the <b>number of times</b> the control failed.</div>
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Substantive procedures</div>Detect <b>material misstatement at the assertion level</b>: tests of details and substantive analytical procedures. Conclusion depends on the <b>monetary value</b> of errors.</div>
</div>

### Generating procedures: AEIOU
| | Audit procedure | Description | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Analytical procedures | Evaluation of financial information by comparison to financial and non-financial data, investigating significant differences and inconsistent relationships | Substantive |
| **E** | Enquiry (and confirmation) | Seeking information of knowledgeable persons inside or outside the entity; obtaining representations directly from a third party | Enquiry: both · Confirmation: substantive |
| **I** | Inspection | Examining records, documents and tangible assets | Both |
| **O** | Observation | Looking at a process or procedure being performed by others | Tests of controls |
| **U** | Recalculation (and re-performance) | Verifying arithmetical accuracy; the auditor's independent execution of procedures or controls | Recalculation: substantive · Re-performance: tests of controls |

### Which source is stronger (ISA 500)
External better than internal · auditor-generated better than client-generated · internal more reliable when controls are effective · documentary better than oral · originals better than copies. *CP1 practice Q9: a bank confirmation received directly by the auditor is the most reliable.*

### The 3W formula (CP4)
Every substantive procedure = **VERB** (inspect, recalculate, reperform, reconcile, agree, trace, enquire, obtain, cast, confirm, vouch) + **NOUN** (name the document: GDN, GRN, supplier statement, aged receivables listing, board minutes…) + **WHY** (the assertion or risk). "Check", "look at", "ensure" score zero. Build procedures in [Checkpoint 4](/c/5/cp4-substantive-procedures/).

### Errors from the records, omissions from outside
<div class="b-grid">
  <div class="b-card" style="border-top:4px solid var(--bisc-rose)"><div class="b-kicker">Tests designed to discover errors</div>Start with the <b>accounting records</b> and go to supporting documents. Detects overstatement, and understatement through causes other than omission.<br/><small><b>Test debit items</b> (expenditure or assets) <b>for overstatement</b>.</small></div>
  <div class="b-card" style="border-top:4px solid var(--bisc-emerald)"><div class="b-kicker">Tests designed to discover omissions</div>Start <b>outside the accounting records</b> and match back. Omissions are never revealed by starting with the account itself.<br/><small><b>Test credit items</b> (income or liabilities) <b>for understatement</b>.</small></div>
</div>

Directional testing derives from **double entry**: a misstated debit means a misstated credit, or an opposite misstatement of another debit. That is why the [Master Map](/c/1/c1-master-map/) works. Choose the direction:

In ACCA wording the verb is often **trace** in both directions; what scores is the **starting point**. Branch A names the two directions *vouching* and *tracing*: [Bài 1.3](/a/1/a1-3-vouching-tracing/).

<TabItem label="5 · Roll-forward">

### Opening + additions − reductions = closing
The slides open the PPE, inventory and payables chapters with the same **account schedule**. Learn the frame once and you have an audit plan for any balance-sheet account.

| Account | Opening | + Additions | − Reductions | Closing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-current assets (Ch 12) | Agreed to signed FS / prior file | Purchase · self-construction completed · revaluation · reclassification | Depreciation · impairment · disposal · reclassification | FA register · physical count |
| Inventory (Ch 13) | Prior year | Purchase · reclassification | Cost of goods sold · impairment · reclassification | Inventory listing · physical count |
| Payables (Ch 16) | Prior year | Purchase · forex revaluation · reclassification | Payment · goods returned · debit notes · reclassification | Supplier listing · supplier statements · reconciliation |

**Each line maps to an assertion.** Opening = accuracy of brought-forward figures (cumulative balances carry errors forward, Ch 12 Activity 2). Additions = existence/occurrence and rights (vouch) plus completeness (search outside). Reductions = valuation (depreciation, impairment, NRV) and completeness of disposals. Closing = existence and completeness against the count, the register or a third party.

> **Ch 12 Activity 2 · Opening balances.** The opening balances should be agreed to the signed financial statements and any breakdown provided by the client to ensure they have been carried forward accurately from the previous year. Normally also agree to the previous year's audit file, but in a first-year audit this is not possible.

The same frame audits provisions (opening, movements, closing: Ch 17 §2.2) and loans (prior-year working papers, repayments in the cash book, new loans in minutes: Ch 17 §1).

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="6 · Proof in total">

### Build an expectation, then compare
ISA 520 analytical procedures as substantive evidence: **variance analysis** (vs prior period or budget), **ratio analysis**, **proof in total** (interrelationships between financial and non-financial data to estimate an expected value). Factors: suitability for the assertion · reliability of the data · precision of the expectation · acceptable difference.

| Balance | Expectation (slides) |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | (Opening cost + closing cost) / 2 × depreciation rate, per category (Ch 12 Activity 6) |
| Payroll | Estimated average wage (last year + expected increases) × average number of employees (Ch 16 §2.1) |
| Finance costs | Loan balance × rate × months, adjusted for repayments (Ch 7 Activity 1) |
| Warranty provision | Provision as % of revenue vs prior year (Ch 17 Activity 2) |
| Rent | Rental agreement × months for each floor occupied (Ch 7 Activity 2) |

**When not to use analytics (CP4):** one-off fraudulent transactions are embedded in totals, so use tests of detail for specific fraud risks; an analytical procedure is only as good as the precision of its expectation.

<KeyPrinciple>**Assertion first, direction second, procedure last:** the risk tells you the assertion, the assertion tells you where to start, and the 3W formula turns it into marks.</KeyPrinciple>

<QuickCheck lang="en" q="An assistant reviews the payables ledger, selects 25 recorded balances and agrees each to supplier invoices. Will this find unrecorded liabilities?" short="No">
Starting from the ledger can only test what is recorded (existence, accuracy). Omissions are found by starting outside the records: post-year-end payments matched back to the year-end payables listing (completeness).
</QuickCheck>
