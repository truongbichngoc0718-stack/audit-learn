**Interim vs final audit:** interim (during the year) → risk, understanding, recording and testing controls, some substantive work · final (after year end) → substantive procedures, confirmations, analytical procedures, subsequent events, going concern, written representations. **Documentation:** enough for an experienced auditor with no previous connection to understand the work and conclusions; permanent file (engagement letters, legal documents, prior signed FS) vs current file; ACCA recommends keeping papers at least seven years.

<Eyebrow>Risk · materiality</Eyebrow>
## From scenario facts to audit risks
**Materiality (ISA 320):** information is material if its omission or misstatement could reasonably be expected to influence users' economic decisions; quantitative and qualitative. Benchmarks: 0.5–1% of revenue · 5–10% of profit · 1–2% of total assets. **Performance materiality** is set lower. Revise as the audit progresses and document everything.

**Ratios as risk radar (Ch 5 Activity 2, Dress You Like Co):**
| Ratio | 20X6 | 20X5 | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin · supermarket | 3% | 8% | Margin collapse on the main customer |
| Receivables days · supermarket | 81 | 62 | Slower collection: recoverability |
| Inventory holding period | 85 days | 35 days | Inventory $13.8m vs $4.9m: NRV and existence |

**Six risks and responses (Ch 5 Activity 1, abridged):**
| Audit risk | Auditor's response |
|---|---|
| New audit: less understanding → higher detection risk | Understand the business fully, brief the team, more experienced staff, test opening balances |
| Fashion inventory may be below cost (IAS 2) | Aged inventory report; discuss saleability; check valuation at lower of cost and NRV |
| Two sites with transfer errors: double or missed counts | Review count instructions; raise weaknesses before the count |
| 60-day credit to the supermarket + worsening cash flow → going concern | Cash flow forecasts and their assumptions; supermarket's ability to pay; access to finance |
| Former FD suing; no provision or disclosure | Correspondence with the director and legal advisers; discuss treatment; minutes and subsequent events |
| No FD since March; internal audit (bank and supplier reconciliations) cancelled | Personnel to answer queries; detailed review of year-end bank and supplier reconciliations; larger samples; post year-end payments |

**Fraud (ISA 240):** fraudulent financial reporting vs misappropriation of assets; fraud needs **pressure/incentive** and **opportunity**. Prevention and detection is management's and TCWG's responsibility; the auditor obtains reasonable assurance the FS are free from material misstatement whether due to fraud or error. Where fraud risk exists: treat as significant, assign and supervise staff carefully, evaluate accounting policies, **build in unpredictability**. **Laws and regulations (ISA 250):** those with a direct effect on amounts (tax, pensions) vs those fundamental to operations (licences, regulatory compliance).

<Eyebrow>Internal control</Eyebrow>
## Five components, known limits
| Component (ISA 315 · CP3) | Core idea | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Control environment | Tone at the top: culture, integrity, ethics | Board oversight, code of conduct, HR policies |
| Risk assessment process | Identify and analyse risks to objectives | Fraud risk assessment, risk registers |
| Control activities | Policies and procedures that address risks | Authorisation, reconciliations, sequence checks, IT controls, segregation |
| Information & communication | Relevant information captured and communicated | Accounting system, management reports, whistleblowing |
| Monitoring | Ongoing evaluation of controls | Internal audit, management reviews, exception reports |

**Limitations:** human error · deliberate circumvention / collusion · management override · unforeseen circumstances · cost vs benefit. Insufficient segregation of duties is a **deficiency**, not an inherent limitation (CP3 practice Q1). **Smaller entities:** limited segregation, override risk → mostly substantive approach.

**Recording systems:** narrative notes (simple, flexible; hard to spot missing controls) · flowcharts (easy to review, show deficiencies; only standard systems) · ICQs/ICEQs (all controls considered, easy to delegate; may miss unusual controls, no weighting).

**IT controls:** **general controls** apply across systems (development and testing of applications, prevention of unauthorised program changes, passwords, back-ups, disaster recovery) · **application (information processing) controls** work on individual transactions (control totals, document counts, one-for-one checks, digit verification, reasonableness, existence and range checks, batch reconciliations, master-file controls).

**Communicating deficiencies (ISA 265):** significant deficiencies go **in writing to TCWG**. Significance depends on susceptibility to loss or fraud, likelihood of material misstatement, interaction with other deficiencies, cause and frequency, volume of activity, subjectivity of estimates, amounts exposed and importance of the control. **ISA 260** also covers the auditor's responsibilities, planned scope and timing, significant findings and, for listed entities, independence. Exam format: deficiency → recommendation → test of control (see [C3.2](/c/3/c3-2-purchases-payables/)).

<Eyebrow>Sampling and automated tools (Ch 11 · ISA 530)</Eyebrow>
## How many, which ones
- **100% testing:** few high-value items, significant risk where nothing else works, repetitive calculations by automated tools. **Specific items** (high-value, key, all over an amount, stratification): not sampling, cannot be projected.
- **Statistical:** random, systematic (constant interval, random start), monetary unit sampling (picks material items; struggles with negative items and non-random order). **Non-statistical:** haphazard, block (used for cut-off).
- **Larger sample when:** higher risk of material misstatement · higher required confidence · higher expected error · lower tolerable misstatement. **Evaluate:** is an error anomalous? Tests of details → project monetary errors; tests of controls → error rate.
- **Automated tools:** audit software (substantive), test data (controls), audit data analytics (examine all data). They extract and calculate but cannot evaluate, e.g. the adequacy of an allowance (Ch 14 Activity 4).
