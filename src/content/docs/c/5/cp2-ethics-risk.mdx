## Ethics – The Two Pillars
| Fundamental principles (ACCA Code) | Threats & safeguards framework |
|---|---|
| 1. INTEGRITY – straightforward & honest | Self-interest threat – financial or other interest in client |
| 2. OBJECTIVITY – no bias or conflict of interest | Self-review threat – reviewing own work |
| 3. PROFESSIONAL COMPETENCE & DUE CARE – maintain knowledge | Advocacy threat – promoting client's position |
| 4. CONFIDENTIALITY – do not disclose without authority | Familiarity threat – too close a relationship with client |
| 5. PROFESSIONAL BEHAVIOUR – comply with laws, avoid actions that discredit profession | Intimidation threat – deterred by threats from client |
| SAFEGUARDS: Professional (education, standards) + Firm-level (rotation, reviews, independence checks) | SAFEGUARDS: Client safeguards (governance structures, audit committees) |

## Specific independence issues
| Issue | Threat type | Safeguard / rule |
|---|---|---|
| Financial interest in client | Self-interest | Dispose of interest; or decline engagement |
| Loan to/from client | Self-interest | Only permitted if on normal commercial terms (bank client) |
| Long association (>7 years – listed entity) | Familiarity | Rotate engagement partner; 5-year cool-off |
| Close family member works at client | Familiarity / Self-interest | Consider significance of role; reassign or decline |
| Providing non-audit services (e.g. tax prep) | Self-review | Management must take responsibility; separate teams |
| Litigation with client | Intimidation / Self-interest | Assess significance; may need to resign |

## Risk assessment
Audit Risk = Inherent Risk × Control Risk × Detection Risk

| Component | Description & exam focus |
|---|---|
| Inherent Risk (IR) | Susceptibility of an assertion to misstatement, BEFORE controls. Higher for: complex transactions, subjective estimates, related party transactions, new accounting standards. |
| Control Risk (CR) | Risk that client's controls do NOT prevent/detect misstatement. Higher when: no segregation of duties, override of controls, manual systems, poor IT controls. |
| Detection Risk (DR) | Risk that audit procedures fail to detect misstatement. The ONLY component auditor CONTROLS. When IR/CR high → reduce DR → more work needed. |
| Audit Risk Model | AR = IR × CR × DR → DR = AR / (IR × CR). If IR and CR both high → DR must be set very LOW → extensive substantive testing. |
| Materiality (ISA 320) | Threshold above which misstatements are considered material. % benchmarks: PBT (5%), Revenue (1–2%), Gross Assets (1–2%). Performance materiality = lower than materiality. |
| Business Risk | Risk to the client entity's objectives. NOT the same as audit risk. Business risks that affect FS reliability become audit risks. |

## Ethics MCQ technique
| If you see… | The answer usually involves… |
|---|---|
| Auditor owns shares in client | Self-interest threat → dispose of interest or do not accept |
| Auditor prepared client's tax return | Self-review threat → different team; management take responsibility |
| Partner has been partner for 10 years (listed) | Familiarity threat → rotation required (7-year rule for listed) |
| Client threatens to withdraw if qualified opinion | Intimidation threat → resign if necessary; cannot compromise opinion |
| "Shall we mention this to management first?" scenarios | Whistleblowing/confidentiality → consider public interest; NOCLAR |

## Risk assessment – Section B writing technique
Each audit risk = 2 marks. Structure: Scenario factor (WHY) → Audit risk (WHAT could go wrong) → IFRS reference if applicable.

| Audit risk (½ p identify, ½ p explain) | Audit response (1 p for appropriate response) |
|---|---|
| **Step 1:** Identify factors that give rise to audit risk (FS level: account balance, transactions, disclosure; Assertion level: completeness, existence, occurrence) | **Step 3:** Suggest appropriate/practical safeguard: describe auditor's approach to assess whether balance/transactions were materially misstated |
| **Step 2:** Explain the implication (state which specific area of FS impact with an assertion – Eg: Overstate Inventory - Valuation) | 3 key responses: • 8 methods + context → inspect documents? Physical check? Inquiry whom? Discuss about what? • 2 general procedures, i.e. ARP / casting, reconciling GL & sub-ledger • Increased sample size / more experienced audit staff + describe the adjustment needed |

**Note:** DO NOT give answer from business perspective INSTEAD OF audit perspective · if 2 more pieces of information result in same audit risk → only 1 point is given · Misstate/Mislead is awarded only when it is clear the balance could be either over or understated; otherwise use Overstate/Understate · the audit response should be answered in the same format as an audit procedure: what to do (verb) + to what (noun) + for what reason · allocate time and detail depending on the marks given.

| Scenario factor | Audit risk identified |
|---|---|
| Company is loss-making / directors have bonus scheme based on profit | Management may overstate revenue or understate expenses to hit targets (misstatement of revenue/expenses) |
| New accounting system implemented during year | Risk of data migration errors; transactions may be processed incorrectly or lost |
| Significant related party transactions | Transactions may not be at arm's length; may not be properly disclosed per IAS 24 |
| Going concern doubt / bank loan covenant close to breach | Inappropriate going concern basis used; disclosures may be inadequate (ISA 570) |
| Inventory held at multiple locations / third parties | Existence and completeness of inventory unverifiable; overstatement risk |
| Director estimates involved (e.g., warranty provision) | Subjectivity in management estimates may lead to material misstatement of provisions |
