## 1.1 Five components of internal control (ISA 315 / COSO)
| Component | Core idea | Examples in practice |
|---|---|---|
| Control Environment | Tone at the top — culture, integrity, ethics | Board oversight; code of conduct; HR policies; management philosophy; commitment to competence |
| Risk Assessment | Identify & analyse risks to objectives | Fraud risk assessment; change management; risk registers |
| Control Activities | Policies & procedures that address risks | Authorisations; reconciliations; sequence checks; IT controls; segregation of duties |
| Information & Communication | Relevant info identified & communicated | Accounting system captures transactions; management reports; whistleblowing channels |
| Monitoring | Ongoing evaluation of controls' quality | Internal audit; management reviews; exception reports; self-assessments |

## 1.2 Sales system
Overall objective: All valid sales are recorded accurately and cash received promptly.

| Stage | Risk | Control objective | Control example | Test of control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Order placed | Low-creditworthy customer accepted | Goods supplied only to customers with good credit rating | Credit check by independent dept; credit limit authorised & reviewed | Inspect credit check docs; test credit limit acceptance process |
| | Unfulfilled orders | All goods ordered are despatched/fulfilled | Sequentially numbered orders; copy to sales dept; open order review | Test numerical sequence; review open orders file |
| Despatch of goods | Goods not despatched to correct destination | Goods despatched to correct destination | Customer signs GDN; copies to customer/driver/warehouse/invoicing | Inspect sample of GDNs for customer signatures |
| Goods invoiced & recorded | Despatched goods not invoiced / invoiced wrongly | All goods despatched correctly invoiced | Sequential invoices; GDN matched to invoice; authorised invoice | Sequence check; agree sample invoices to GDN and price list |
| | Invoice posted to wrong customer account | Invoices posted to correct accounts | Monthly customer statement reconciliations; review unpaid amounts | Reperform reconciliation on sample; review complaint log |
| Payment received | Cash not received | Money received for all invoices raised | Aged receivables report produced periodically; pursue overdue balances | Review ageing report; trace cash receipts to bank statement |
| | Cash misappropriated | Money safeguarded and banked promptly | Segregation of duties: receivables updater ≠ invoice raiser ≠ cash handler | Observe banking procedures; check segregation in practice |

## 1.3 Purchases system
| Stage | Risk | Control objective | Control example | Test of control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Order stage | Unauthorised order placed | Orders placed only once authorised, for business purpose | POs raised only by authorised individuals; preferred supplier list | Review sample of orders for authorisation evidence |
| | Items not bought at competitive price | Items bought at most competitive price | Preferred supplier list; separate quotations for non-standard items | Vouch order to preferred supplier list; inspect quotation files |
| Goods received | Ordered goods not received → stockout | All goods ordered are received | Sequential GRNs; unmatched orders reviewed periodically | Observe receipt of goods; verify GRN matched to order |
| | Faulty goods accepted | Only high-quality goods accepted | Goods inspected for condition on receipt; employee signs GRN | Observe receipt process; inspect GRN for quality sign-off |
| Invoiced & recorded | Goods received not recognised in system | All liabilities recognised accurately | GRN copies to accounts; 3-way match GRN ↔ Invoice ↔ Order | Trace GRN vs invoice; verify accruals made for unmatched GRNs |
| Payment made | Payment to wrong supplier / duplicate payment | Suppliers paid accurately and to correct party | Invoices authorised for payment; stamp 'PAID'; supplier reconciliations | Inspect invoices for authorisation; reperform supplier reconciliation |

<Eyebrow>2 · Exam techniques</Eyebrow>
## 2.1 Answer structure — Deficiency → Recommendation → Test of Control
<div class="b-grid">
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Control deficiency (½ p identify + ½ p explain)</div><b>Step 1:</b> Pick the fact from scenario — spot what is MISSING or WRONG vs best practice. Do not copy the scenario text.<br/><b>Step 2:</b> Explain the risk — be specific about what could go wrong and the financial impact.<br/><small>✗ Bad: "No credit checks are performed" — this is just copying the scenario.<br/>✓ Good: "Goods may be supplied to customers who are unable to pay, leading to irrecoverable debts and overstatement of receivables."</small></div>
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Control recommendation (1 p)</div><b>Step 3:</b> Phrased as a specific, practical ACTION. State WHO does WHAT and HOW. Never use 'ensure that…'<br/><small>✗ Bad: "Ensure credit limits are enforced."<br/>✓ Good: "The system should automatically block sales orders where the customer outstanding balance exceeds their approved credit limit; any exceptions must be reviewed and approved by the credit controller before despatch."</small></div>
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Test of control (1 p)</div><b>Step 4:</b> Test the SPECIFIC recommended control — not a general audit procedure. Verbs: Inspect / Observe / Enquire / Reperform / Recalculate.<br/><small>✗ Bad: "Check credit limits" — too vague, no detail.<br/>✓ Good: "For a sample of sales orders, inspect the system log to confirm the automatic block triggered for orders exceeding the credit limit; for any overrides, agree to evidence of approval by the credit controller."</small></div>
</div>

## 2.2 Checklist — scanning the scenario for controls
| Control type | Question to ask when reading scenario | Example (Sales / Purchases cycle) |
|---|---|---|
| Authorisation | Is there someone approving this? Who? At what level? What is the threshold? | Credit limits authorised by finance director; POs approved by manager before submission to supplier |
| Segregation of duties | Is one person doing two incompatible functions? (Maker / Checker / Recorder / Custodian) | Person who records sales ≠ person who handles cash receipts; AP clerk ≠ cheque signer |
| Information processing | Is there a check that data input/output is valid, complete and accurate? | GDN matched to invoice before raising; 3-way match (PO ↔ GRN ↔ Invoice) before payment |
| Performance reviews | Is there a comparison of actual vs expected? By whom? How often? Followed up? | Sales director reviews monthly revenue analysis by store; investigates unexpected variances |
| Physical controls | Is there physical security, restricted access, asset safeguarding? | Warehouse access restricted; CCTV; inventory stored in locked area |
| IT / Application controls | Are there system-enforced checks, access rights, exception reports, automated blocks? | System blocks order if customer exceeds credit limit; exception report on supplier master file changes reviewed monthly |

<div class="b-flags">
  <div><b>Confuse tests of control with substantive procedures</b>Tests of controls test whether a CONTROL is operating. Substantive procedures test whether a BALANCE is misstated. Never mix them.</div>
  <div><b>Vague procedures score zero</b>'Check credit limits' scores zero. State WHAT document, HOW you check it, FOR WHAT purpose.</div>
  <div><b>Recommendations phrased as objectives</b>'Ensure all invoices are authorised' scores zero. Must be an action: 'The finance manager should approve all invoices above $5,000 before payment, evidenced by signature.'</div>
  <div><b>Copying the scenario as the deficiency</b>The deficiency is often what is NOT described. 'A report is generated and filed' — the deficiency is that no one REVIEWS it.</div>
  <div><b>Direct control explanation too vague</b>'This will prevent fraud' scores zero. Explain specifically what misstatement the control prevents or detects.</div>
</div>

## Part B · Granstan Co
12 marks · approx. 25 minutes · Source: December 2024 Exam — Question 1.

<div class="b-card x-cr" style="margin-top:.6rem">
<p><b>Background.</b> It is 1 July 20X5. You are an audit supervisor with Flinda &amp; Co and you are working on the audit of Granstan Co, which sells electronic kitchen appliances from 40 stores across the country. You are reviewing documentation provided by the finance director of Granstan Co, including extracts of the company's system of internal control, in advance of the interim audit for the year ending 30 September 20X5.</p>
<p><b>Integrated systems note.</b> Although the website, payroll and inventory systems are stand-alone, the sales and purchases modules are fully integrated into the computerised accounting system.</p>
<p><b>Sales — Credit customers.</b> Granstan Co has both business and retail customers. Business customers can apply for a credit account through Granstan Co's central credit control department. After performing necessary checks, the credit control department sets a credit limit which is authorised and then regularly reviewed by the finance director.</p>
<p><b>Sales — Online and in-store purchases.</b> As well as purchasing in store, business customers can purchase goods via the company's website and collect them from their nearest store or have them delivered. As part of the invoicing process, a member of the finance team matches the details on the online order with the goods dispatched note (GDN) before the sales invoice is raised based on these documents.</p>
<p><b>Sales — Discount scheme.</b> Each store charges the same prices for goods but customers who are on the mailing list receive weekly emails with discount offers. To claim the discount, customers must show this email at the cash tills (cash registers) and have the discount barcode within the email scanned. The system then deducts the discount from the price charged.</p>
<p><b>Sales — Management review.</b> Granstan Co's monthly management accounts analyse revenue by store, product and type of customer. Percentage movements in each of the categories are reported, with comparison to the previous month and the previous year. On a monthly basis, the sales director reviews this information, investigates any unexpected movements and completes a report which is presented at the next board meeting.</p>
<p><b>Purchases — Supplier master file.</b> Granstan Co has a central purchasing department. A monthly exception report of any changes made to the supplier master file data is automatically generated, printed and then filed.</p>
<p><b>Purchases — Ordering.</b> When goods are ordered, a purchase order is generated within the purchase ordering module, authorised by the relevant individual and sent to the supplier. The purchasing and finance departments as well as the stores are able to access the ordering module to view orders.</p>
<p><b>Purchases — Goods received.</b> Goods are delivered directly to the relevant store where a store employee checks the quantity supplied to the supplier's delivery note and the purchase order. On completion of the checks, an electronic goods received note (GRN) is produced. Only the stores and the finance department have access rights to view the completed GRNs within the module.</p>
<p><b>Purchases — Supplier reconciliations.</b> Due to staff shortages in the finance department, supplier statement reconciliations are no longer performed.</p>
