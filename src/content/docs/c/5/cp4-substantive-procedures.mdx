## Financial statement assertions: what they mean and how to test them

| Assertion | What it means | Key procedures | Common trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Existence / Occurrence** | Recorded assets/transactions actually exist or occurred | Physical inspection (book → floor); third-party confirmation; post-year-end cash receipts | Trace direction: book→floor = existence. floor→book = completeness. Never mix them. |
| **Completeness** | All assets/transactions that should be recorded ARE recorded | Trace floor→book; sequence checks on pre-numbered documents; post-year-end payment testing for liabilities | Completeness of liabilities is often MORE at risk than existence. Test both directions. |
| **Valuation / Accuracy** | Assets recorded at correct amounts; transactions at correct values | Recalculate; agree to invoices/contracts; review aged listings; NRV assessment; allowance review | Recalculate = auditor does the maths. Agree = compare to source document. Both needed. |
| **Cut-off** | Transactions recorded in the correct accounting period | Agree GDNs around year end to invoice dates; agree GRNs to accrual dates; review pre/post year-end journal entries | Revenue cut-off: test from GDN date, NOT invoice date. Performance obligation determines timing. |
| **Rights & Obligations** | Entity has the right to assets; obligations are the entity's own | Inspect title documents; legal confirmations; loan agreements; confirm no charge over assets if needed | Often combined with existence — but they are different: you can exist without having the right. |
| **Presentation & Disclosure** | Amounts are correctly classified and disclosed per accounting standards | Review disclosures against IAS/IFRS checklist; confirm classification in FS | Always include a disclosure procedure — exam frequently awards a mark for this. |

## The 3W formula: how to write a full-mark procedure
Every substantive procedure must contain three elements. Missing any one element loses marks.

| Element | What it means | Strong verbs / examples | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VERB** (What to do) | The audit action — must be specific and professional | Inspect · Recalculate · Reperform · Reconcile · Agree · Trace · Enquire · Obtain · Cast · Confirm · Vouch | Check / Look at / Ensure / See if — too vague, score zero |
| **NOUN** (To what) | The specific document or item being tested — name it exactly | Sales invoice / GDN / GRN / Payroll sheet / Supplier statement / Aged receivables listing / Service contract / Board minutes / Bank statement | "Supporting documents" alone — must name at least one specific document |
| **WHY** (For what purpose) | The assertion being addressed or the risk being tested — link to the scenario | To confirm existence / To test cut-off / To verify arithmetical accuracy / To assess recoverability / To ensure compliance with IAS X | Can be omitted only when the purpose is self-evident (e.g. cast the listing). Usually include it. |

**Full mark vs half mark vs no mark** (R&D context)

| Mark | Example Procedure |
|---|---|
| ✅ Full mark | Obtain the GL and select a sample of development cost additions. Trace each to supporting documents (invoices, payroll sheets, timesheets) to verify the existence and accuracy of the capitalised amount. |
| ◐ Half mark | Obtain the GL and select a sample of development cost additions. Trace each to supporting documents. (No purpose stated — why are we tracing? What assertion?) |
| ✗ No mark | Perform vouching procedures on R&D costs. (Too general — no noun, no purpose. Which documents? What are we checking?) |

## Standard procedures by account area
These procedures appear across many exam questions. Know the pattern, adapt to the scenario. Click a tab.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Non-current assets">
- Reconcile FA register to GL and financial statements · Cast the register for accuracy
- Inspect physical assets for a sample of additions (existence) · Trace a sample of physical assets to the register (completeness)
- Agree cost of additions to supplier invoices / architects' certificates · Check capital vs revenue distinction
- Recalculate depreciation and compare to client's charge · Review useful lives and residual values for reasonableness
- Inspect title documents / land registry / invoices to confirm rights and ownership
- For disposals: agree proceeds to sale documentation; recompute profit/loss on disposal
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Inventory">
- Attend the inventory count: trace test-counted items to final listing (existence) · Trace listing to count sheets (completeness)
- For count performed before year end: review continuous records for movements to year end
- Obtain final inventory listing; cast and agree to GL and financial statements
- Review for slow-moving, damaged or obsolete items · Obtain NRV evidence (scrap quotes, post-year-end sales) and compare to cost (IAS 2)
- Agree unit costs of a sample of items to recent supplier invoices (cost accuracy)
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Receivables">
- Cast the aged receivables listing and agree total to GL and FS
- Send direct confirmation letters to a sample of customers · Follow up non-responses
- Review post-year-end cash receipts: cash received after year end confirms existence and collectability
- Review aged listing for old/overdue balances · Inspect correspondence with slow payers · Assess adequacy of allowance for doubtful receivables
- Agree year-end balances to sales invoices and GDNs (cut-off and occurrence)
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Revenue">
- Perform proof-in-total analytical procedure (e.g. units × price = expected revenue); compare to recorded amount
- Select GDNs around year end; agree despatch date to revenue recognition date (cut-off)
- Trace a sample of revenue transactions from sales order → GDN → invoice → ledger (completeness, occurrence)
- For long-term contracts: obtain percentage of completion evidence; recalculate revenue under IFRS 15
- Review credit notes raised post-year end for evidence of returns or adjustments relating to the current period
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Payables & accruals">
- Obtain payables listing; cast and agree to GL and FS
- Reconcile a sample of supplier statements to the purchase ledger accounts
- Test for unrecorded liabilities: review post-year-end payments and match to year-end accruals
- Trace a sample of payable balances to purchase orders, GRNs and invoices (existence and accuracy)
- Perform analytical procedures: compare payables days to prior year and industry norms
</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Payroll">
- Trace a sample of employees from payroll to HR records and employment contracts (occurrence — confirm real, current employees)
- Recast payroll records and agree net pay to bank transfer summary
- Recalculate gross-to-net pay for a sample of employees (agree deductions: PAYE, NIC, pension)
- Review payroll for joiners and leavers around year end (cut-off)
- For bonuses/directors' emoluments: agree to service contracts; recalculate; confirm disclosure per local legislation
</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Question types

<div class="b-grid">
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Type 1</div><b>Describe substantive procedures for a specific account area or transaction class</b><br/>Answer structure: Start with an overall/analytical procedure → then test-of-detail procedures targeting specific assertions flagged in the scenario (e.g. completeness and cut-off of income).</div>
  <div class="b-card"><div class="b-kicker">Type 2</div><b>Which TWO procedures best address a specific risk?</b><br/>Read the requirement carefully — it will specify the assertion or risk. Eliminate options that address a different assertion. Prioritise procedures that are direct, evidence-rich (third-party &gt; internal &gt; analytical).</div>
</div>

## Common exam mistakes
<div class="b-flags">
  <div><b>Procedure too vague to score</b>'Vouching to supporting documents' scores zero. Name the document. State the purpose. Use an ISA verb.</div>
  <div><b>Wrong direction of testing</b>Book → floor = existence. Floor → book = completeness. Mixing these is a fundamental error.</div>
  <div><b>Test of control instead of substantive procedure</b>Tests of control test whether a CONTROL works. Substantive procedures test whether a BALANCE is correct. Never mix them.</div>
  <div><b>Cut-off tested using the invoice, not the GDN</b>Revenue cut-off must be tested from the despatch date (GDN), not the invoice date. Performance obligation is satisfied on despatch.</div>
  <div><b>Analytical procedure used for fraud items</b>Analytical procedures are ineffective for one-off fraudulent transactions — they are already embedded in the totals. Use tests of detail for specific fraud risks.</div>
  <div><b>Forgetting the disclosure procedure</b>Exam questions frequently award one mark for reviewing disclosures against the relevant accounting standard checklist. Always include it.</div>
  <div><b>'Increase professional scepticism' without a specific procedure</b>This scores 0.5 marks alone. Must be followed by a specific additional test: 'focus on testing X by performing Y.'</div>
  <div><b>Management representations as primary evidence</b>Written representations are supplementary evidence, not a substitute for procedures the auditor could perform directly.</div>
</div>
